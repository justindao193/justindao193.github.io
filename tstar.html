Helping shy and commuter students make friends in a big cohort with TStar

In one term, our team of five researched, sketched and tested a mobile app for low-pressure friendships at U of T. I ran the survey, sketched the quest screens and ran tests. A year later, I redesigned the app and made this film.

The problem

Students meet plenty of people, but do not feel connected

Big lectures, long commutes and networking-style events make it hard to turn a classmate into a friend. Our survey showed how common that is.

How might we help shy and commuter students start friendships, without the pressure?

Ideas

We ranked every idea by impact and effort

Final design · 2026

I redesigned TStar on my own to answer every open question

Find common ground

Shared interests light up, so you know what to say.

Do a quest together

Scan a buddy’s code, post a photo, and fuel the cohort rocket.

My decisions

Why I made these calls

Every change in the redesign started with something a tester did or said.

→
01

One action to say hi, not two

Saw
Testers could not tell “Shoot a Star” and “Send a message” apart.
Did
Merged them. A Star carries an optional note and opens the chat right away.
→
02

Explain points where people ask

Saw
One tester asked, “What does going to the Moon do?”
Did
Added a 3-line “How Star Points work” sheet, one tap from the rocket card.
→
03

Never block a quest on a dead phone

Saw
If a buddy’s phone is dead, nobody can scan.
Did
Added “Add them by name” so the quest still counts.
→
04

Stop mistakes before they happen

Saw
The only slip in testing was posting before adding a photo.
Did
The post button stays off until a photo is added.
→
05

Give people something to say

Saw
Testers said starting a chat after a Star felt dry.
Did
Added quick replies like “Want to grab lunch after class?” so the next message is one tap.

Product thinking

How I would know it works, and keep it safe

If TStar launched, these are the numbers I would watch and the guardrails I would build in from day one.

Success funnel

  1. Stars sentAre people saying hi?
  2. Chats startedDoes a Star turn into a talk?
  3. Quests doneDo they meet in real life?
  4. Back in week 2Does it stick?

Safety built in

  • Only people in your cohort can see you
  • Block and report on every profile
  • “Open to chat” is a switch you control
  • Ignoring a Star sends no notice, so there is no rejection

Why not just use…

  • Discord or group chats. Easy to join, but big and noisy. Shy students stay quiet.
  • Clubs and faculty events. Need time on campus and feel like networking.
  • Bumble BFF. Strangers from anywhere, not people in your classes.
  • TStar. Only your cohort, a tiny first step, and a reason to meet.

Reflection

What I learned

The hardest part was never the screens. It was understanding how nervous people feel before they say hi, and designing around that feeling.

01

Calm beats features

Matching, games, live alerts→One small, simple action

Taking pressure away helped more than any extra feature.

02

Clear beats clever

“Blastoff”→Names that say what they do

All three testers asked what “Blastoff” meant.

03

Flow is everything

Star, then inbox→Star, then straight to chat

One extra screen can undo a good idea.

04

Gentle design for shy users

Points for everyone→Fun parts stay optional

Quieter students should never feel pushed.
